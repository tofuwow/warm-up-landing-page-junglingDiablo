About don't doubt!

The junglig diabolo is a circus instrument made by two sticks, a rope that brings together the two sticks, and the axle.

One technique to use the diabolo is by placing the axle in front of the right foot (if you are right-handed) and make it roll to the other side using the sticks (the rope must have some pressure because of gravity).

Once you arrive on the left foot, you must pick it up, here the magic happens, maintain the left hand straight and the right on motion, is like fishing!

Voilà the magic! The diabolo moves!

History

The Diabolo is derived from the Chinese yo-yo encountered by Europeans during the colonial era. However, the origin of the Chinese yo-yo is unknown. The earliest mention of the Chinese yo-yo is in the late Ming dynasty Wanli period (1572–1620), with its details well recorded in the book Dijing Jingwulue by the Liu Tong. The book refers to Chinese yo-yos as "kong zhong" (simplified Chinese: 空钟; traditional Chinese: 空鐘; pinyin: kōng zhong; lit. 'air bell').

Diabolos are made of different materials and come in different sizes and weights.

There are many names in the Chinese language for the Chinese yo-yo:

  1. simplified Chinese: 扯铃; traditional Chinese: 扯鈴; pinyin: chě líng; lit. 'pull bell sound'
  2. simplified Chinese: 响簧; traditional Chinese: 響簧; pinyin: xiǎng huáng; lit. 'sounds like a reed (instrument)'
  3. Chinese: （抖）空竹; pinyin: dǒu kōng zhú; lit. '(shaking) sky bamboo'
  4. simplified Chinese: 空钟; traditional Chinese: 空鐘; pinyin: kōngzhōng; lit. 'sky bell'
